The reality check

Can we actually build this?

Yes, in stages. Here is every requirement you gave me, rated honestly. The hard parts are marked so they don't ambush you later.

  • Easy

    Headphone jack

    An audio chip and a 3.5 mm socket. Clicks' Communicator reportedly has one and the official Unihertz Titan 2 page says it has none, so yours is a feature, not a fossil.

  • Doable

    Physical keyboard

    A custom key board with small switches under clear keycaps. The keyboard-nerd world already has the tools: KiCad, QMK firmware and a very good PCB guide.

  • Doable

    SIM card

    Buy a 4G modem module that already has a SIM socket. Do not design the radio yourself. Ever.

  • Doable

    Linux

    Runs on cheap boards today, and phone-ready Linux exists (postmarketOS, Mobian). You will have a working prototype months sooner than with Android.

  • Hard

    Android

    Needs a chip maker's board package. The Play Store needs Google's licence, which takes months. Start Linux, run Android apps with Waydroid, or use an Android module later.

  • Doable

    See-through 90s shell

    Clear resin prints for prototypes. Injection-moulded tinted polycarbonate for the real thing. Tint beats perfectly clear (see the alerts).

  • Boss fight

    Voice calls on your own hardware

    Some carriers only let approved phones make calls over 4G (VoLTE). Texts and data come first, calls second, and your country decides how hard it gets.

  • Boss fight

    Selling it

    A radio device needs legal certification (FCC, CE and friends) plus carrier tests. Think tens of thousands. Pre-certified modules shrink the bill but do not erase it.

The plan

Hold it

about 2 weeksabout $60 to $150looks-like model0/6

Done whenYou are holding a see-through, phone-shaped thing and you know whether the size feels right.

  • How

    Tap the dots under the phone at the top of the page and tell me your top two in chat. The first print uses your pick.

    Tinted resin is the 90s look, so choose a colour you can live with.

  • How
    1. Grab the two shell files from chat: sheer_front_shell.stl and sheer_back_shell.stl.
    2. Open one quote page and upload a file: JLC3DP, PCBWay SLA printing or Xometry.
    3. Choose SLA resin and the transparent or clear material. Quantity 1.
    4. Screenshot the price for both shells and paste it in chat.

    PCBWay's own guide recommends their UTR-8100 transparent resin for clear parts.

  • How

    Pick the cheapest transparent SLA option, one of each shell. If polishing or a clear coat is offered, add it. If tinted clear resin is offered, choose your colour from step 1, otherwise order clear and tint it later.

    Allow one to two weeks with shipping. The walls are 2.6 mm thick, which clears the usual 1.2 mm print minimum.

  • How

    The four sheer_dummy_*.stl files are fake parts. Any cheap opaque print works: a friend's printer or a library makerspace is fine. Paint the battery yellow and the board in your colour, then tuck them inside the shells.

  • How

    Search for a used BlackBerry Q10 on eBay or a local marketplace. You are buying it to learn what good thumb typing feels like and to borrow its key layout. Do not pay extra for pristine.

  • How

    Close the shells around the dummy parts, hold it next to the Q10, and write down three things you would change. Send them to me and I will update the model.

Make the guts work

4 to 8 weeksabout $250 to $400works-like prototype on Linux0/9

    Tell me in chat. It decides which 4G bands the modem needs and whether voice calls are realistic where you live. Look for your carrier's page about bringing your own device and about VoLTE.

  • How

    Pick a cheap prepaid SIM with data and texts. Before buying, ask the carrier whether the SIM works outside a phone (hotspot or modem). Some lock the SIM to a phone's IMEI.

  • How

    Everything is on the shopping list. Check each live listing first, and check that Waveshare ships to your country.

  • How
    1. Install Raspberry Pi Imager on a laptop.
    2. Flash a microSD card with Raspberry Pi OS. Turn on SSH and Wi-Fi in the settings.
    3. Connect the touch display, boot, and see the desktop.
  • How

    Fit the HAT, insert the SIM, power up, then run mmcli -L in a terminal. You want to see a modem listed. Waveshare's wiki page has the connection details and ModemManager is the software.

    Nothing shows up? Copy the exact error into chat and we debug it together. I can't touch your hardware, but I can read logs and write fixes.

  • How

    Text yourself from the command line (ModemManager's docs show how). Then set up a mobile-data connection with your carrier's APN and open a web page. Both working means the radio is real.

  • How

    Plug headphones into the Pi 4's 3.5 mm jack and play a sound. Then use the headset jack on the 4G HAT for call audio tests.

  • How

    Place a call with AT commands through the HAT. If your carrier requires VoLTE approval for unknown devices it may be refused. Read Bullshit Alert 3 and move on if so.

  • How

    Use any USB mini keyboard to try the layout from the model: four rows, ten keys wide, a D-pad, call and end keys. Note what is missing.

Bullshit alerts

The stuff that eats months.

Hardware dreams usually die in the same few places. I'd rather you hear it from me now.

Alert 1

Clear plastic is not free glamour

Every wire, glue blob and logo sticker is on show, and perfectly clear plastic looks cheap fast. All the old gear you sent is tinted translucent. Pick a tint, design the guts to look good, and treat the battery label like artwork.

Alert 2

Android on your own hardware is hard mode

You need a chip maker's board package, and the Play Store needs Google's licence plus device testing, which sources put at months. Start on Linux, use Waydroid for Android apps, and revisit Android modules at Stage 3.

Alert 3

Calls may be refused in your country

Some carriers only let approved phones place voice calls over 4G (VoLTE). PinePhone owners hit this in the US in 2021. Get text and data working first, then ask your carrier, or a reseller that doesn't care what device you use.

Alert 4

2G and 3G modules are dead ends

Cheap GSM boards look tempting, but 2G and 3G networks are closing in many countries. Buy 4G LTE. The first ZeroPhone started on 2G, which is charming history and a bad plan.

Alert 5

Selling a radio needs paperwork

Rough figures from vendor blogs: FCC testing in the thousands, PTCRB around $10k to $50k, plus lab time for the whole phone. A pre-certified module helps but does not certify your finished phone. Building one for yourself needs none of this. Also, don't take pre-orders until a prototype works.

Alert 6

The software is most of the work

Sleep and wake, modem state, audio routing and battery life are where phones fall apart. Budget more time for software than hardware. This is the part I can help with the most.

Alert 7

Lithium batteries in clear cases show their feelings

A swelling pouch cell is visible from outside. Use a protected cell, keep screws away from it, and know that shipping batteries has its own rules (UN38.3). Never puncture or squeeze it.

Alert 8

Tooling is the big first bill

A simple prototype mould can start around $1,500, but forum and vendor estimates put a polished, tinted two-part phone shell at $10k to $50k. Get quotes from two moulders before you fall in love with a shape.

Design notes

Choices I made for you.

Say the word and I'll change any of them.

Tinted, never fully clear

Real 90s see-through gear is tinted. A tint hides some mess and makes the guts look intentional.

The guts are the artwork

A coloured circuit board with gold pads, a yellow battery label and four chrome screws. We design the board to be looked at.

Jelly keys that glow

Backlit keys bleed colour through clear keycaps, and the notification light glows through the plastic.

Square screen, BlackBerry style

A 56 × 56 mm window leaves room for a roomier keyboard. Fewer pixels, more thumb comfort.

Headphone jack on top

With a tiny printed headphones glyph, like a Walkman. A headset-capable jack, so mics work too.

Visible screws, repairable

Four screws and no glue. Repairable is a selling point, and it looks right on see-through tech.

A pet in the status bar

A small Tamagotchi-style pet that reacts to battery and typing. Cheap software, great for screenshots.

Swap-on jelly backs, later

90s phones had snap-on covers. Selling extra backs is merch money for version 0.2.
